About

Policy research, spatial analysis, and public-facing visualization.

I am a justice policy researcher and John Jay graduate student whose work focuses on mass incarceration, prison oversight, racial equity, public safety, and community violence prevention. My portfolio brings together policy research, spatial analysis, and public-facing data visualization to translate complex legal and empirical issues into accessible tools for advocacy, research, and public understanding.

I am currently completing an M.A. in International Crime and Justice at John Jay College of Criminal Justice, where my academic work focuses on public safety, crime mapping, comparative criminal justice, racial equity, and evidence-based interventions. I previously earned an M.A. in Human Rights from John Jay, graduating with distinction and receiving the inaugural Human Rights Excellence Award.

My work is shaped by graduate research training, direct-service experience, policy advocacy, and lived experience with the criminal legal system. I use public data, spatial analysis, and policy research to examine how justice systems distribute harm, resources, accountability, and protection across communities.

NYC Shooting Redistribution Dashboard

Visualizing Uneven Public Safety

NYC Shooting Redistribution by Neighborhood, 2016–2025

This interactive HTML dashboard analyzes shooting redistribution across New York City Neighborhood Tabulation Areas from 2016 to 2025. Users can select baseline and comparison years, filter by borough or change category, and hover over each neighborhood to view shooting change, citywide share shift, race/ethnicity population context, and employment context.

The dashboard defaults to 2021 and 2025 to compare the post-pandemic shooting surge with the most recent full year of incident-level data. It is designed to show how citywide public safety trends can obscure uneven neighborhood-level exposure to gun violence.
